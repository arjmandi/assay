Table 0 — RHAE, the benchmark's own score

Read this before the other tables. Games-cleared, levels-cleared and total-actions are coverage and cost statistics, not scores. RHAE scores each level as min(115, 100·(baseline/actions)²), each game as the index-weighted mean of its levels capped at 100 — but an unfinished game gets weighted progress only, with no efficiency credit at all. Inefficiency is penalized quadratically; surplus efficiency is capped away. The formula was derived from published scorecard JSONs, and rhae.py --validate reproduces 25 of 25 published game scores exactly (worst error 0.000000). ASSAY's figure is computed from its journals, not asserted — and then confirmed by the benchmark itself: our consolidated verification-replay card 702ccd4f returns 96.54%, matching per game on every row (177/183 levels, 24/25 environments, 8,157 actions). That card is a replay of recorded action sequences, so its wall-clock is machine replay time, not agent time.

Axis starts at 90 — the four scores span 90–101, and a zero-based axis would flatten them into one indistinguishable block. Dashed line: the ARC-reported human-expert baseline (95.4). lf52 alone costs ASSAY 3.27 RHAE points — over its 24 won games ASSAY scores 99.80. That one unfinished game outweighs every efficiency difference in Table 1 combined, because RHAE gives no credit for finishing under baseline.
