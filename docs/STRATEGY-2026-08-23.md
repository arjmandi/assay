## 2. The reframe: sell trust, not capability

The capability edge is real but marginal (dead heat with arc-skill single-session; ~11% cheaper than Prime Agent) and it decays with every model release and every copied idea — the user's own instinct ("copying and changing ideas is very cheap these days") is correct. The integrity position is categorical and appreciating:

- **What only ASSAY has:** enforced predict-before-act; code-graded claims; hash-chained, externally anchored journals; `assay audit` recomputing integrity from artifacts alone; gates (destructive/approval/budget/liveness with rehearsal quotas); FOREIGN demotion of imported knowledge; VACUOUS-verifier detection; a proposal lane the agent cannot self-ratify.
- **Documented failures it answers:** Prime Agent bypassing FLE via raw RCON despite explicit anti-cheat prompts ("the hole was capability, not policy"); FLE's chest-stuffing holdout; Sakana's system reward-hacking its own eval sandbox; every self-reported harness number on OOLONG having no citation-integrity check. Environment-integrity enforcement is a recurring, expensive, unsolved problem — and it is ASSAY's entire thesis, already written in bench/factorio/RESEARCH.md §4.
- **Who pays for it (from the market dossier):** labs buying RL environments (Epoch rate card: $200–$2k/task, up to $20k complex; six-to-seven-figure quarterly vendor contracts; 4–5x exclusivity premium; Anthropic reportedly >$1B/yr appetite [unverified]); UK AISI licensing "state-of-the-art agent scaffolds" (£3k–£15k/task) and granting up to £1M/project; METR treating scaffold quality as measurement input; Fleet $1M→$60M ARR; Mercor $2B run-rate buying environment startups; Prime Intellect's own hub of 400+ community environments with immature verification.
- **Positioning sentence:** *ASSAY is the referee layer that makes agent results provable and agent deployments governable — demonstrated at frontier capability on ARC-AGI-3, at a measured ~8% overhead.*

Two standard-setting conversations become available under this framing, neither requiring code release:
1. **ARC Prize Foundation** — their community leaderboard demands full code because they have no other integrity mechanism; an auditable-journal standard is an alternative they need more as harness numbers saturate.
2. **Prime Intellect Environments Hub** — an integrity layer / "certified un-hackable environment" service for their 400+ community environments.

## 3. Disclosure: the layered answer to "should I open-source?"

Think in four layers, not one switch:

| Layer | Contents | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Verification surface | journal format spec, standalone audit/replay verifier, claim-grammar spec | **Open early.** It creates the trust asset (anyone can verify ASSAY journals), contains no capability sauce, and is the seed of a standard with your name on it |
| 2. Benchmark parity kit | adapters, registries, PROTOCOL.md per bench | Open as needed for reproducibility/parity claims |
| 3. Kernel | gate, grading engine, chains, gates/daemon | **Closed.** This is the sellable product for the integrity market |
| 4. Capability sauce | DOCTRINE.md, memory/agency machinery, model tier, batching law, playbooks | **Closed longest.** The most copyable and least protectable part |

Notes:
- The repo currently mixes all four layers ("this repo is only for code and bench results we might open source it"). **Split before any release** — DOCTRINE.md in particular is the single most copyable file in the repo; an "open the code" decision made casually would ship the doctrine with it.
- Precedents (disclosure dossier): Poetiq opened one thin config, kept the meta-system, got verified, raised $45.8M eight weeks later. NVIDIA published AVO with no code. MindsAI stayed closed, forfeited prize money, became Tufa's core team. The pattern the user suspected is real and respectable. The counter-pattern: every *unverified* results-only claim (Sakana, Reflection 70B) was dismantled within days. ASSAY's server-issued scorecards + shareable replays + triple verification already meet the accepted no-code credibility standard — this is rare and dissolves most of the open-source-for-credibility pressure.
- You can always open later; you can never close after opening.

## 4. Impact: the paper is the product launch

The tech report / paper has three claims none of the 100% announcements made:
1. **The referee protocol and its measured cost** — full audit integrity at 7.8% exploration tax and ~zero win-rate cost, under caps, domain-neutral. "Integrity is nearly free" is the quotable result.
2. **The exercised-claim finding** — six impossibility proofs, each consistent with every recorded transition, each wrong precisely where no transition had gone; the coverage protocol (enumerate load-bearing rules → audit what exercised each → buy graded probes for gaps) converting five into wins within existing budgets. This is a novel, general contribution about agent epistemics under verification. **Name the protocol** — named findings travel. It is also the most stealable idea in the eventual paper, which is an argument for publishing sooner.
3. **Honest cost/efficiency accounting** — measured $ per run, probe/batch split, n=1 discipline, honesty notes. Retrodict's $654 made cost a headline axis; ASSAY has better instrumentation of it than anyone.
4. **RHAE is saturated at the frontier** — quantified 2026-08-24: the 115/level and 100/game caps compress the top of the leaderboard into a completion checklist. A 12% raw-action gap between the two leading Opus systems is worth ≈0 RHAE (both sit at the caps nearly everywhere), while one unsolved game costs 3.27 points. The axes that still discriminate — integrity, measured cost, information tier, caps — are exactly the ones ASSAY instruments. This also quantifies the roadmap: the machinery phase (lf52, +3.27 available) is worth more than all possible efficiency work combined (~0 available).

Sequence: publish the scorecard batch (already planned "in one batch at paper time") + tech report on arXiv + the layer-1 verification tools, together. That single release is simultaneously: the credibility event, the funding collateral, the ARC paper submission (if scoping works), and the first brick of the standard.

Then **Factorio/FLE** (M0–M2 per the dossier, then the 24-task sweep): the anti-cheat experiment (capability boundary vs prompt-level anti-cheat, with Prime Agent's incident as the published foil) + the world-model-divergence question (FLE's dominant failure mode is exactly what predict-before-act meters measure) + upside if any of the 7 never-solved tasks falls. Cost per the dossier: $50–200 for a single-attempt sweep; runs on this laptop. This turns "good at ARC-AGI-3" into "general referee, two domains," which is what both funders and buyers need to see. OOLONG E-C7 and the goal-framed corpus benchmark are the second paper, not the first.

## 8. Risks, honestly

1. **The idea leaks via the paper.** Yes — publishing methods at principle altitude means the exercised-claim protocol and predict-before-act gating will be copied. That is what publishing is; the alternative (sitting on it) risks someone else naming the finding first, which is strictly worse. The kernel/doctrine stay unpublished; velocity on the referee roadmap is the real defense.
2. **Solo + employed velocity.** The field went 30.2% → 100% in four weeks. Score races reward velocity you don't have part-time; protocols, verification, and findings reward rigor, which is the demonstrated edge. The strategy picks battles accordingly — but the half-time decision cannot be postponed forever; set a tripwire (e.g., first $25k of grants or first pilot LOI → negotiate hours).
3. **Verification asymmetry.** One unverifiable public number and the whole trust position dies (Sakana precedent). The existing discipline — never claim what the scorecard/journal doesn't show — is the brand; keep it absolute.
4. **Employment IP.** Until the letter exists, everything else is provisional.
5. **ARC paper-track scoping** may come back demanding more openness than acceptable — the paper still ships (arXiv), only the prize is lost.
