§ BenchmarksTwo papers · three worlds

One harness, benchmarked in three worlds.

The Assay paper ran Claude Opus 5 on ARC-AGI-3, the Factorio Learning Environment and OOLONG under one kernel, with a graded prediction on every paid action. The RSI paper then ran Claude Haiku 5.5 on ARC-AGI-3 in a loop that rewrote the harness twice from Haiku's own record. Every number below names the run that produced it.

Study 01The Assay paper · ARC-AGI-3 · Claude Opus 5

25 games, then three experiments on the same kernel.

The campaign played each of the 25 public games once under hard caps, with a graded prediction on every paid action. Three experiments then asked what carried the result: the gate, the record, and the record carried into a new run.

The campaign: 25 games, once each, under hard caps.

Inputs were fixed at tier-2 information: the constitution, a registry with one-word action hints, a sentence that the observation is a 64×64 grid, the number of levels and the action cap of 200, 500 or 1,500. No game-specific strategy, no curriculum, no network.

The set is server-scored: a consolidated replay of the 25 recorded action sequences produced public scorecard 702ccd4f, on which the benchmark's own scoring returns 96.54 with 177 of 183 levels, 24 of 25 games and 8,157 actions.

Four published systems sit at or within a fraction of 100. The paper does not argue rank. What distinguishes the Assay row is the record under it: every one of the 8,157 paid actions carried a graded prediction, every journal is clean under the public checker, and every win is triple-verified.

E1: the gate enforced, lifted, and the instrument removed.

Three games, two seeds, three arms: gated (gate: required, the constitution), ungated (gate: optional, the instrument available and the manual silent on it), and removed (gate: off, the instrument refused, a manual with the discipline cut out). Eighteen runs, eighteen wins.

On every game the two seeds of one arm differ by more than the means of two arms differ. The pre-registered reading is the first one: wins equal, actions in band, so at this sample size the enforcement is not what carries the behavior.

One thing did move. Agents whose manual never taught the discipline still attached a prediction to every paid action once the instrument was there. What they predicted differed: 836 of the ungated arm's 1,405 graded outcomes were the explicit change, against 28 of 1,491 in the gated arm, where 1,054 were verifier outcomes. The manual decided how specific the prediction was.

E2: the five proofs, resumed without an operator.

On six occasions across five games the campaign agent concluded, with an argument and in one case an exhaustive search over 8.2 million states, that a level was impossible. Every proof was consistent with every recorded transition, and every proof was wrong in the same way: a load-bearing rule had never been exercised in the regime where the proof needed it.

Each archived proof state was rebuilt from the read-only journal, cut at the proving session's last paid action, and resumed twice by fresh sessions under one neutral prompt: once with the coverage-audit module, once without. The prompt said nothing about coverage, impossibility, the proof or any module.

Ten runs, ten wins. All five proofs fell in both arms. The module fired 26 times, every time as a loop breaker, and never named a rule a proof rested on. The correction was carried by the record: the inherited notes named the untested rule, the journal held the event ids that showed where it had and had not been exercised, and a fresh reader found it. This is the paper's central result.

E3: carryover of the agent's own record, under forced demotion.

A run exports its notes with the lines marked verified, a digest of its graded outcomes, its hazard tags, up to twenty verifier programs with their statistics, and its formal model. A later run imports that artifact as foreign: every verified line lands as assumed, every verifier as a candidate that earns standing only by being named in a prediction and graded again, the model with no rights.

Two games, one import run each, from the game's own gated seed-1 run. ft09 is a null cell: the import run read the prior notes, named none of the twenty imported verifiers, admitted 74 of its own and finished in exactly the source run's count. tr87 is one large effect: 119 actions against 324 and 216 cold, with 129 fresh verifiers and none of the imported ones used.

In both runs the FOREIGN discipline held as designed. What transferred on tr87 was the prose understanding in the notes, re-earned through fresh predictions and fresh code. Two cells, direction only.

Study 02The Assay paper · Factorio and OOLONG · Claude Opus 5

The same kernel in two worlds with no grid.

Generality is only checkable across worlds. The kernel, the constitution and the protocol of the ARC-AGI-3 campaign were attached to a build-and-optimize world and to a long-corpus question set, through the registry and the adapter alone. Each is a small bounded sweep, not a scored benchmark, and each is reported with its cost.

Factorio: the agent's code is the action.

The Factorio Learning Environment is a build-and-optimize world. The adapter registers two actions: RUN, whose one parameter is a Python program run against the environment's API, and WAIT, which advances the world by an exact number of ticks.

Before a program runs, the adapter screens it with an abstract-syntax allowlist, denied names and attributes, and a ban on identifiers that start with an underscore. A refused program still spends the action and is journaled as a policy refusal, so the agent sees the refusal as an observation. The win predicate also requires a real producing entity, so a forged production statistic cannot score a win. This is world policy, and it lives in the adapter.

Three lab-play throughput tasks ran under a cap of 64 paid actions. All three were won: iron plate in 5 paid actions for $1.99, iron gear wheel in 9 for $4.87, electronic circuit in 9 for $4.44. Every journal was clean under both checkers, and the screen refused no program.

Predictions on a world with no grid

The iron-plate run's fourth action was a WAIT, predicted as

verify:checks/window_ok.py; ch tick = 3960; ch windows delta >= 1;
ch automated crosses 16 from below; ch producer = True; change

and its fifth and last as

win; level+1; verify:checks/window_ok.py; ch tick = 7560; ch windows = 2;
ch automated delta >= 60; ch producer = True

The circuit run's last action:

win; level+1; ch gtick delta = 3600; ch nwin delta = 1; ch lastrate = 20 ± 5

A rule like “one measurement window has passed and automated output crossed the quota” is not a sentence in the notes. It is a crossing outcome on a named state that the kernel computes from the world's response, and when it holds it becomes a fact with its actual value.

Why the screen is in the adapter.

A rule in a prompt competes with the task for the model's attention, and under enough pressure a prohibition can lose outright. In a published incident, an agent told not to cheat on this environment reached its admin console and spawned resources into its own factories.

Here the refusal is code that runs before the program does. No agent attempted that reach in these runs, so the fail-closed property rests on construction, on the refusal test and on one scripted probe in a smoke run, not on an incident. The broker and the adapter were both patched during the phase for faults the agent could not see, so the kernel was unchanged in its agent-facing behaviour rather than byte for byte.

In the October replay, the circuit run's six extra actions went to a residual the August run had already recorded (a pole route that stops short of wire reach), to an inserter placed one tile off, and to a boiler that ran out of fuel. With one run per task, the replay says the October build reproduces the August behaviour on this world, and nothing finer.

OOLONG: when the gradable thing is not the thing that matters.

OOLONG asks questions whose answers need the whole corpus: counting, cross-referencing and timeline comparison over every record. The adapter writes the corpus to a file in the run directory and keeps it out of the observation, so the model's context never holds it.

BANK_FACT commits a fact with a span that the adapter checks verbatim against the corpus; a span that is not there is refused and journaled. SUBMIT answers the current question with cited spans and is coverage-gated by a census of banked facts. Answers are sealed and scored only at the end, by the benchmark's own scorer, so correctness never enters the journal during the run.

Four arms ran on Claude Opus 5 over the same corpora, questions and scorer. The raw model walls past 128K, since the larger corpora exceed its context window. The three harnesses land in one band, well above it, at every length. And on a fair footing Assay does not lead on accuracy and is the most expensive harness: weakest of the three at 128K, where its counting questions collapsed without the hint; tied at 0.740 at 1M for $48.76 and 114 minutes against Claude Code's $8.20 and 30; tied at the top at 4M for $2.40.

The 1M rung does not discriminate.

At 1M the per-question score vectors of the three harness arms are identical: the same four counting questions score zero for every arm, and 19 of 25 answers are byte-identical across arms. The tie says nothing about the harnesses and everything about the corpus and its questions.

Each rung also draws on a different source corpus, so the table reads across a rung, not down a column. What the sweep supports is narrower: accuracy holds across lengths where the raw model cannot run at all, the verification layer (span-checked facts, coverage gating, sealed scoring, a hash-chained journal) exists in only one arm, and the gated path as built costs several times what a plain harness costs at 1M.

E5: where the cost sits.

E5 kept the kernel and its discipline and changed only the actuator: several spans per BANK_FACT and a plain-text SUBMIT, with cost bands fixed before launch. The run answered all 25 questions at the same 0.740 with the same per-question vector, in 50 paid actions with every prediction held and no refusal, for $43.39 and 76 minutes against $48.76 and 114.

The journal's timestamps place the cost. The first paid action came 68 minutes after the start, and all fifty took seven and a half minutes. In both Assay runs the offline phase, where the kernel imposes nothing, went to classifying all 20,321 reviews through the model; the Claude Code agent hand-labelled 2,655 and trained a bag-of-words classifier for the rest. The actuator shape is ruled out; the strategy chosen under the Assay framing remains, and one run per arm cannot attribute it.

One sentence, moved between layers.

The OOLONG registry's goal text carried one strategy sentence: the corpus is a file in the run directory and should be read with the agent's own offline tools. Removing that sentence, and nothing else, made the de-hinted arm.

At 1M the two arms scored within one question of each other, 0.701 hinted against 0.740 de-hinted, and their cost moved from $5.16 to $48.76. At 128K the sentence was worth 0.870 against 0.764, and without it the counting questions collapsed. A sentence at the registry layer changed cost by close to an order of magnitude and capability on one class of question, with the kernel and the constitution untouched.

Verifiable and non-verifiable work.

The gate needs a world that answers. On a grid or a tick counter the answer is immediate and crisp, and the discipline is cheap: on ARC-AGI-3, 8% of paid actions were single probes. On OOLONG the world is a corpus and correctness is sealed until the end, so the harness can grade evidence integrity during the run (a span is verbatim, a question has a banked fact) and cannot grade the answer.

The OOLONG cost table is what the gated path costs when the gradable thing is not the thing that matters. For tasks whose success cannot be checked in code at all, the design offers the record and the instruments, not the gate.

What these two sweeps do not show.

Factorio is three tasks with no control arm, and its fail-closed claim is untested by incident.

OOLONG is one corpus per rung with a different source per rung, and its 1M rung does not discriminate between harnesses.

Both are reported to show the kernel attaching unchanged to worlds unlike ARC-AGI-3, with the cost in full, not to rank harnesses on them.

Study 03The RSI paper · ARC-AGI-3 · Claude Haiku 5.5 · 2026-10-25 to 10-28

Recursive self-improvement for ARC-AGI-3: hill-climbing to 78.95 RHAE with Haiku 5.5.

A strong analyst model read every Haiku run through the harness's layers and recommended code changes where each loss pointed. The author adopted every recommendation, tagged a new version, and ran again. Because ARC-AGI-3 verifies a win, each won game stays pinned to the version that won it, and the newest version attacks the rest.

Each version runs, explores its losses, and is read by an analyst.

ARC-AGI-3 verifies a win: the engine reports it, and the journal's chain head fixes the action sequence that produced it. So a won game is pinned to the version that won it, and a pin moves only to a later win with a higher RHAE. Every game not yet won goes to the newest version, first at medium effort, then at xhigh on the games it lost.

Between versions, Claude Fable 5.1 ran an analysis of about 120 agents over every journal, note, receipt and thinking trace. Each lost game got a case study, six lenses read across all runs, and skeptics tried to refute every finding. They refuted 21 of 36 lens findings the first time and 17 of 36 the second.

The loop is human-in-the-loop. The author adopted every recommendation without selection, approved the patches a coding agent wrote, chose the ten games pass 2 explored, and made three operator changes no analysis recommended: a guard for actions sent in GAME_OVER, a pinned tool list, and an explicit effort flag.

Never won in six passes: bp35, dc22, ka59, lf52, sp80, su15, tn36, wa30. Their losses on fix 2 have one shape: an audited stop, accepted by the gate, whose audit names rules that were tested and misses the one that mattered.

Haiku did not run out of budget. It stopped.

On the unmodified harness, all 26 Haiku losses ended by Haiku's own judgment, with most of the budget unspent, and all 26 conclusions were wrong: Sonnet cleared every one of those levels, 14 of the 20 medium stop levels in 55 actions or fewer. Fourteen were impossibility proofs consistent with every graded transition and wrong on a rule never exercised. Twenty-one stops named the untested probe in their own notes and left it unbought.

Haiku's model fit every transition it tested and was wrong in regimes it never tested. On the levels it stopped on, its predictions were too vague for the harness's meter to see the error: on tn36's fifth level, 205 of 234 predictions were a bare change, none of 224 world-model outcomes missed, and all 19 gambles missed.

Analysis 1 found the levers above the model: the player prompt licensed the stop, the batch runner never installed the shipped Stop gate, and the conclusion gate keyed on a word Haiku never wrote. Fix 1 moved the stop one session later.

Analysis 2 found the exits fix 1 had opened. The gate's count rule never held a first session (21 of 25 first-session stops accepted at medium), held sessions edited the old STOP block in place and looped, and the new sentence “Play until WIN or the cap” made the cap the exit: 10 of 11 cap runs were blocked first and then spent 7,758 actions. Fix 2 closed those exits.

Against Claude Code alone, and against chance.

The baseline played the 25 games with Claude Code and no harness, through a thin game command, under the same budgets except that a game could use five sessions against the harness passes' three. For Sonnet the unmodified harness is worth 3 games and 11 RHAE points. For Haiku it made no measurable difference: 5 wins either way, +2.7 RHAE (interval −8.9 to +14.6). The gain is the harness as the analyses changed it.

Nor is the gain just not stopping. The baseline relaunched up to five sessions a game and spent 14,849 actions, more than fix 2's 12,028, yet completed 97 levels and 5 games against fix 2's 128 and 12.

A random agent under the same engine, rules and 1,500 actions scored 0.68 RHAE with uniform clicks and 0.83 clicking only non-background cells, over five seeds each, with no win and 2 to 7 of 183 levels.

Under the same version, the evidence is partial: a repeat of fix 2 at medium, stopped after ten games, won four of the five earlier wins among them again and lost cn04 at 5 of 6 levels. Five wins are too few for a re-win rate.

Designed on the games it was scored on

The analyses read the 25 public games the next pass played, with Sonnet's solved records as the account of each game's mechanics. No change names a game, and each is marked world-agnostic or grid-specific; transfer to unseen games is untested.

One run per cell

Run-to-run variance is not measured. m0r0 was won, lost at the cap, and won again across versions. The intervals cover the sampling of games only.

Pins assume a re-win

Across versions, 11 of 13 medium wins were won again, and every lost win came back at xhigh. Under one version only a partial repeat exists, four of five.

No ablation, a human in the loop

Each round shipped its changes together, so no change's share is measured. The author adopted the changes, chose pass 2's games and made three operator changes.
